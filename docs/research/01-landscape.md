# News-analysis product landscape

## 요약

- 2026-09-15 기준으로 글로벌 5개 현행 서비스와 Artifact의 종료 사례, 한국 5개 서비스를 조사했다.
- 요약·기사 묶음·개인화만으로는 차별화하기 어렵다. Ground News, Particle, Naver가 이미 조합해 제공한다.[1][7][33][35]
- 글로벌 비교에서는 출처 비교와 읽기 도구, 한국 비교에서는 포털 접근성과 쉬운 설명·읽기 습관이 각각 중요한 기준이다.[1][16][33][44][49]
- 추천 가설은 **근거를 확인할 수 있는 사건 페이지 + 마지막으로 읽은 이후의 변화**다. 시장 공백이 확정됐다는 뜻은 아니다.
- 한국어로 시작하고 한국 관련 국제 이슈에 영어 원문 비교를 붙이는 범위를 우선 검증하되, 최종 시장 선택은 제품 책임자에게 남긴다.
- 편향 점수는 사실 검증과 다르다. Ground의 미국 정치 기준을 한국에 그대로 적용하지 않아야 한다.[2]
- 7개 차별화 후보를 난이도·포트폴리오 효과·시장별로 비교했다. 초기에는 근거 연결과 변화 추적에 집중할 것을 권한다.
- 읽은 위치 복원, 명확한 알림 설정, 오류·최신성 표시, 접근성, 실제 결제 단위는 제품 완성도의 검증 항목이다.
- Artifact의 8인 팀도 지속 투자를 중단했다. 기능 수보다 반복 사용 가치와 운영 범위를 먼저 검증해야 한다.[25]
- 가격·기능은 확인한 판매 채널과 문서 날짜를 구분했으며, 팀 규모와 확인하지 못한 기능은 미확인으로 남겼다.

## Scope

**Observation date: 2026-09-15; cutoff: that date, not the entire future month.** The supplied issue brief defines a solo-developed general-news analysis web app and a portfolio for full-stack and AI/ML roles. This is research, not an approved implementation plan.

The sample contains five active global products, one historical global case, and five Korean products. Ground News, Particle, Google News, Naver News, and Daum are direct reading/discovery comparators. Feedly and Readwise Reader are adjacent monitoring/reading tools; DeepSearch is an adjacent B2B intelligence product; NEWNEEK and Longblack provide editorial and habit-design comparisons. They are not interchangeable markets.[1][7][11][16][22][33][40][43][44][49]

**Evidence rules:** live official pages establish what vendors currently document, not independently measured quality. Dated announcements establish historical availability only. Pricing is the displayed currency/channel/billing period, not a tax-inclusive personalized checkout quote. “Unknown” means not established by the inspected sources, never proof of absence. Company-size bands, named team rosters, and product-team headcounts are different quantities. No paid accounts, native-app walkthroughs, accuracy benchmarks, or checkout purchases were used.

User comments are first-person evidence of a complaint, not representative surveys or proof of a present defect. Date-qualified older reports remain useful failure scenarios. Recommendations and difficulty estimates below are researcher judgments; citations identify their evidence, not validation of the proposed solution.

**Ground pricing is locale-sensitive.** The earlier native-web fetch displayed the USD amounts above; a later same-day fetch of the same checkout URLs displayed **Premium EUR 32.99/year and Vantage EUR 102.99/year**. The cause/visitor country was not established. These are observed displays, not currency conversions or a universal offer; confirm the actual currency and annual total at purchase.[3][4]

**Ground's categories require careful interpretation.** Its bias and factuality ratings apply to publishers, not individual article truth. Its bias framework is explicitly situated in US politics, and unrated sources do not appear in the bias bar. Therefore a balanced-looking bar cannot establish claim accuracy, independent corroboration, or a valid Korean political classification. The current methodology also specifies a low-factuality coverage limit for Blindspots; older complaints must not be presented as the current algorithm's complete definition.[2]

**Feedly has two different compression operations.** Its documented deduplication uses more than 85% content overlap and checks close duplicates within 31 days; clustering groups differently written coverage of one event. This matters for this project: ten copied reports should not be presented as ten independent confirmations, and related reporting should remain inspectable rather than disappear.[12][13] The latter sentence is a design inference, not a measurement of Feedly's error rate.

#### Artifact postmortem: what is known versus inferred

CEO Kevin Systrom's **2024-01-12** explanation was that “the market opportunity isn’t big enough to warrant continued investment in this way.” The same post reports eight staff and explicitly ties removal of new comments/posts to the moderation and oversight they needed.[25] Yahoo's **2024-04-02** announcement subsequently describes acquiring the personalization technology for integration into Yahoo, with the founders advising during transition.[27]

**Inference for a solo portfolio:** a polished personalized news reader can create reusable technical value without demonstrating a sustainable standalone business. A social layer adds a real operating burden; ship comments only if the reader problem specifically requires them. This does **not** establish that moderation caused Artifact's shutdown or that every news product has too small a market.[25][27]

## Recommendation

**Validate a Korean-first, evidence-backed event reader with “what changed since my last visit” as its central repeat-use job.** Preserve general-news categories, but use a bounded source set and Korean-related international stories for the first evaluated demonstration. This is a portfolio-focused judgment; the research does not settle the target market or prove willingness to pay.

1. **Lead with candidates 1 and 2, plus the online portion of 6.** Demonstrate one coherent loop: open an event → inspect source-linked statements → read an original → return without losing position → revisit and see material changes. Citation UX has existing precedents, and related-story grouping is already baseline; the value must come from a reliable combination and measured outcomes.[12][13][18]
2. **Use candidate 3 as provenance first.** Expose source, publication time, original language, evidence gaps and duplicate relationships. Consider Korean political classifications only after the owner chooses a defensible rubric and a maintenance process; the US publisher-label precedent does not supply one.[2]
3. **Add finite personalization, then selective bilingual comparison and alerts only if interviews support them.** Avoid launching every candidate at once. Existing Particle and Naver capabilities make generic summarization a weak portfolio centerpiece; Artifact illustrates why sophisticated features alone do not establish a viable reader habit.[7][33][35][25]
4. **Make quality inspectable in the demonstration.** Proposed evaluation: a labeled set of multi-source events across the chosen categories; clustering errors and copied-source counts; claim/citation support; correct handling of changed/retracted facts; Korean/English entity matches; response latency and per-event processing cost. These are suggested measurements, not achieved results or industry thresholds.
5. **Set operational boundaries before scaling.** Choose source/content permissions, evidence retention, a daily ingestion/model budget, and a stale-data fallback. Public APIs are integration surfaces, not assumed licenses to republish articles.[54][55][57] Keep a visible correction/report route. A social-comment system should wait for a demonstrated need; Artifact explicitly described its moderation burden.[25]

The portfolio should let a reviewer observe both strengths: **full-stack engineering** through stateful, accessible, recoverable reading flows, and **AI/ML engineering** through reproducible retrieval/clustering/change evaluations and visible uncertainty. This is a suggested bar for this project, not a hiring guarantee.

## Open questions for the interview

1. Which initial reader has priority: Korean general readers, Korean professionals following overseas events, or an English-speaking global audience?
2. What repeated job should the first release win: a five-minute daily briefing, comparison of contested claims, or tracking changes to ongoing events?
3. Is this primarily a portfolio demonstration, a free public service, or a business expected to support paid subscribers? What operating budget is acceptable?
4. Which general-news categories and initial source set are acceptable? Is politics required immediately, and who owns source selection and corrections?
5. Should the product classify political bias at all? If yes, who approves the Korean rubric, handles unknowns/disputes, and maintains labels?
6. How much bilingual scope is essential: interface localization, translated summaries, or actual cross-language source/context comparison?
7. What content agreements or permitted feeds can the project use, and how long may source passages and offline content be retained?
8. Which behavior would make the owner consider the initial experiment successful: repeat visits, accurate recall of changes, successful source verification, or willingness to pay?
9. Are accounts and saved read state required in the first public experience? Which alert channels and acceptable notification frequency should be tested?

Marrenland has said it will wait for a formal reply from Northern Union negotiators after President Halvard Oste turned down a plan to reopen the Strait of Kessel to cargo traffic within ten days.
Earlier, Oste told journalists at the Ashgrove Palace that the plan from Varos was not "workable" and that its leaders wanted an arrangement to open the strait at once because "their position is collapsing".
On Thursday at the Concord of Nations, Lindqvar told journalists that a settlement was possible if "the required terms are honoured" by both governments.
Lindqvar said the terms for reopening the strait matched those set out in a framework accord between the Northern Union and Marrenland that was initialled in April.
Marrenland said it had brought up reopening the strait and a halt to naval clashes in talks held alongside the Concord assembly in Port Linden over the past week.
The narrow channel, which normally carries roughly a quarter of the region's grain and fuel shipments, has been largely shut since fighting broke out in March.
"I'm turning their plan down," Oste said on Friday, adding that the Northern Union already held "full command" of the strait, with "huge volumes of cargo" moving through it.
Lindqvar wrote on Relay that Oste "has offered many hopeful remarks, and just as many contradictory ones, which sadly we have grown used to".
"We are expecting the mediators to pass on the final positions to us, and we will decide on that basis," Lindqvar said.
Talvia, which has served as a go-between for the Northern Union and Marrenland, urged every side to keep talking openly to end the standoff.
Talvia's foreign minister, Osric Delamere-Vane, told the Concord general session that "every party" should work with Talvia to keep shipping safe in the contested channel.
Speaking to the Harbor Broadcast Network on Friday, Pellisier said Marrenland had put forward "partial steps" and "hollow offers meant to impress foreign audiences".
Separately, Finance Secretary Maud Iverholt said on Friday that trade pressure from the Northern Union was straining Varos.
Writing on Quill, Iverholt said Talvia, Brennmoor and the Sorrel Isles had suspended flights by several Marrenland carriers, and some lenders in Brennmoor and the Sorrel Isles had frozen payments to Marrenland.
The Oste administration has named its trade embargo on Marrenland "Operation Closed Harbor", and is using penalties to push Varos to halt strikes on ships in the Strait of Kessel.
